DFU Vision — before you take a photograph

A prototype by Dr Naim Bin Abdul Malek

This demonstrator classifies diabetic foot ulcer photographs with a deep learning model. It is for education and Centre of Excellence demonstration only. It does not replace clinical assessment, probe-to-bone testing, vascular assessment or microbiology, and must not be used to make treatment decisions.

A model is only as good as its inputs. Photographs taken under different distances, lighting and angles are the single biggest source of error in wound image AI. Please follow the standardised image acquisition protocol:

Standardised image acquisition protocol

  • Prepare the wound: remove the dressing, irrigate with saline and gently blot dry before photographing.
  • Distance and angle: hold the camera 30–40 cm away, perpendicular to the wound surface; the ulcer should fill roughly one third of the frame.
  • Lighting: even, diffuse room lighting; flash off; avoid shadows, glare and coloured light.
  • Background and scale: plain drape or sheet behind the foot; place a disposable paper ruler or colour reference beside (not over) the wound.
  • Framing: include a margin of peri-wound skin; no faces, name bands, documents or identifiers in the frame.
  • Consistency: same device, same settings and same view at every visit so change over time is real, not photographic.

    Model card (abridged)

    Approach
    Two models run in sequence, both transfer‑learned from ImageNet and both executed in the browser, so no image leaves the device. Segmentation: a MobileNetV2‑UNet trained on the FUSeg expert wound masks outlines the ulcer and measures it. Classification: a MobileNetV2 with two softmax heads, trained on wound crops taken from those masks, then fine‑tuned over its top 30 layers.
    Why segmentation comes first
    On the FUSeg validation split the ulcer occupies a median 1.3 % of the photograph. Grad‑CAM reads a 7×7 grid, so no cell resolves finer than about 2 % of the image, and the heatmap's peak fell inside the true ulcer in only 13 % of 190 held‑out images. Cropping to the segmented wound raised severity accuracy from 84 % to 92.5 %. Segmentation answers "where"; classification answers "what".
    Explainability
    The wound outline and its measured area are the primary explanation. Grad‑CAM on the final convolutional map (out_relu) is retained as a sanity check on whether the model reads the wound or the room, and is drawn only inside the crop it actually explains.

    Labels — important caveat
    Demonstrator labels are weak labels derived from expert wound masks (wound area for depth/severity) and wound‑bed colour features (slough, eschar for infection). They are not clinician‑adjudicated or culture‑confirmed. Step 3 and the Train tab exist precisely to replace them.

    Organise images into one folder per class, for example wagner_0 … wagner_5, or 1A, 1B, 3B for Texas, or infected / not_infected. The class name is read from the folder. Images stay on this device unless you choose Google Drive.

    Folder upload works on desktop browsers and most Android browsers. On iPhone use "Add per class".

    Data governance. Your DrPH protocol commits to storing images on an institution‑hosted, password‑protected server under the PDPA 2010. Consumer Google Drive is not that. Use this option only for de‑identified images, and prefer the institutional server or the Colab notebook run under an institutional Google Workspace account.

    Transfer learning on this device

    frozen backbone · new head

    Every image is passed once through the ImageNet‑pre‑trained MobileNetV2 backbone to obtain a 1,280‑number embedding. A new classification head is then trained on those embeddings, here in the browser. A stratified 20 % of images is held out to estimate performance. Fine‑tuning the backbone itself needs a GPU: use the Colab notebook.
