## 2. What changed since 9 November

### v10.5 — the estimate, and what the app says about itself
The transfer-learned probability is shown as a percentage, named as an estimate from training
data rather than a finding, and placed **below** the criteria count, so the order on screen is
the order of the evidence. "The model declined to classify this image" is gone: it was a
statement about the model printed where a reader takes it as a statement about the wound. Every
verdict now carries a standing line that the wound and the patient still need to be seen.

### v10.6 — what is and is not a sign of infection
Panel 2b was titled "Signs of infection" and listed six rows, two of which are not signs.
Slough is in most chronic ulcers and is not an IWGDF criterion; dry necrosis and eschar are
tissue death, usually ischaemic, and it is **wet gangrene** that signals infection. The panel is
now **"Findings from the photograph"** with two groups, tissue viability and signs of infection.
The necrosis row reads **"Necrosis or eschar"**, not "dry": the detector is `L < 30`, and dry
eschar, wet gangrene, shadow and an underexposed photograph all land in it, so claiming dryness
would assert the one distinction the method cannot make.

The peri-wound layer also stopped contradicting its own row. The row reported a ring mean
against +3 a\*; the layer painted any cell above 0 at a floor opacity of 30. Half of any ring is
above its own average, so the picture showed red while the row said "Not seen". One named
threshold is now read by the row, the criteria count and the layer.

### v10.7 — erythema measured as an extent
IWGDF/IDSA define the sign as erythema reaching **more than 0.5 cm** from the margin. The old
measure could not express that: a proportional band about 0.15 cm wide, summarised as a mean
that an unaffected majority drags below threshold. Now a Chebyshev distance transform, a 2.5 cm
search band, reference skin beyond it, and a breadth-first search keeping only the
above-threshold region **contiguous with the margin**. Redness still above threshold at the band
edge reads "at least" that distance.

Three consequences to know about:

- **A fourth row state.** Without a ruler scale the distance has no units, so the row reads
  "Set a scale" and the criteria count treats erythema as unassessed, not absent. This is
  stricter than before and deliberately so, but it has not yet been used on a ward.
- **The ruler is no longer study-team only.** Gating it meant the app instructed clinicians to
  press an invisible button and left one of the five criteria permanently unanswerable. This
  reached production; see `docs/clinical-ai-prototype-playbook.md` section 3.9.
- **The grading case definitions had to match, and now do.** "Erythema present?" became
  "erythema beyond 0.5 cm?", the guideline wording but a narrower question, so the definition
  was written against it in `docs/case-definitions.md`. Had the graders labelled the old
  question while the app answered the new one, the kappa would have measured agreement between
  two different questions.

### v11.0 — Wagner, Texas and the antibiotic became separate tabs
One list of nine questions served all three, so reaching a Wagner grade meant answering four
Texas-only questions first. Most clinicians use one scale. Wagner now asks 5, Texas 4, the
antibiotic 8. Each question already carried a `req` tag naming which definitions read it, so
the split is a filter over the existing spec, not a second copy of it — a duplicated list is
where the two would silently drift apart. The answers stay in one object, so nothing is
answered twice and the header says how many carried over. The rule card, the antibiotic panel
and the outstanding count follow the tab; the chosen tab is remembered. The pressure-injury arm
gets NPIAP stage and Antibiotic.

### v11.1 — two defects in the verdict banner
`.verdict b` is the headline rule and the basis line also used `<b>` for a tab name, so the
word "Grading" rendered as a 25 px serif block mid-sentence. And the unassessed-criteria clause
carried its reason in a relative clause before " and " joined the list, producing "erythema
extent, which needs a ruler scale and purulent discharge not assessed".

### v11.2 — the model may no longer set the headline
`infected` read `criteria >= 2 || severe || model >= 0.5`. On a wound with no visible criteria
the panel printed **LIKELY INFECTION** directly above its own line reading **0 of 5 seen**.

The reasoning for letting the model escalate was that escalation is the safe direction to be
wrong in. Three facts defeat it here. The infection head is trained on **weak labels** derived
from the same images' masks and colour features, so its number estimates a proxy computed from
the picture rather than the condition. It is **uncalibrated** — nobody has shown that of 100
wounds scored 76 %, 76 are infected. And **four of the five IWGDF criteria are not in the
pixels**, so no architecture can supply them. A high score with no criteria seen is therefore a
correlation of unknown cause, not a finding the criteria missed.

The model now **prompts rather than concludes**. A score at or above 0.5 with the criteria unmet
produces AWAITING YOUR EXAMINATION and a named line — "the model rates this image 76 % — an
estimate from training data, not a finding, and not enough to call infection on its own, so
examine before discounting it" — placed *after* the criteria count, never before it. It still
cannot de-escalate, so nothing the criteria catch stops being caught, and `pubInfected` no
longer goes true on a model flag alone, so the provisional antibiotic line follows the criteria
rather than the model.

This is the safer direction, not the timid one. The cases given up are ones whose correct next
step was an examination anyway; against that, a false infection call spends antibiotics,
sometimes an admission, and eventually the clinician's attention. It is also the stronger
position for the thesis: the model's output is logged per case, which is what makes calibration
and a prospective comparison possible. That is how it earns the headline later.

Verified across four states: model 76 % and 95 % with no criteria (both AWAITING), two criteria
with the model at 5 % (LIKELY INFECTION, "2 of 5 criteria, at the threshold"), and abscess alone
at 5 % (LIKELY INFECTION, severe on its own).

### v11.3 — the estimate says what it estimates
Four words in the verdict panel had no referent on screen: *criteria*, *the model*,
*transfer-learned*, and the training data. Naming them turned up something worse than the
naming problem.

**The infection label is defined as slough or necrosis.** `ml/make_labels.py`:

```python
inf = "suspected" if (slough > 0.10 or necro > 0.15) else ("none" if ... )
```

Those are the two findings the app tells the clinician are **not** infection criteria. So the
head largely restates the slough and necrosis percentages printed directly above it, and the
panel was showing one measurement twice while inviting the reader to count it as two
witnesses. Peri-wound erythema — the one criterion it might legitimately have learned — was in
the v1 rule and was removed because healthy granulation is also red (held-out AUC 0.72).

Changes:

- **"Transfer-learned estimate: 76 % likelihood of infection"** → **"Image-pattern estimate:
  76 %"**, followed by what it was trained on and the sentence *"not independent evidence of
  infection"*.
- **An expander, "What is this estimate, and what was it trained on?"**, with backbone,
  ImageNet transfer, dataset and citation, the exact label thresholds, what the label was
  *not* derived from, what was left out of it, the measured AUC with the note that it was
  scored against the same proxy, the calibration note, and the guideline behind the five
  criteria. **Every figure is read from `metrics.json` and `calibration.json` at render time.**
  Nothing is written into the HTML, because a hardcoded figure is how the old wording outlived
  the label it described.
- **"0 of 5 criteria seen here"** → the count is now stated over what was assessable. This
  screen can reach two of the five; the other three are named as requiring clinical
  examination and *not being in any photograph*. "0 of 5" read as *five were checked and all
  were negative*, which was untrue by omission.
- The label rule itself was **moved into both model cards** (`label_rule`), so the UI can state
  it without anyone remembering to copy thresholds out of the training script.

**A correction to the v11.2 entry and to what was said in session.** The infection head is not
"uncalibrated". The calibration file shows 4-view TTA brings the reliability slope to 0.96, and
that Platt scaling was fitted (scale 0.956, shift −0.572) and deliberately not applied because
it gave no Brier gain. It is well calibrated — **to the proxy**. The file's own caveat stands:
refit on clinician-adjudicated, culture-confirmed data before any clinical claim.

### The release check is now a browser check, not a grep
`docs/release-check.mjs`. Run it against a working copy or against the deployment:

```
python3 -m http.server 8777 --directory dfu      # or --directory . to serve /dfu/
node docs/release-check.mjs
node docs/release-check.mjs https://htpn-clinical-checker.vercel.app
```

It resolves whether the app sits at `/` or `/dfu/`, passes the entry gate, loads the models,
runs every DFU sample, opens the provenance table, reaches the infected verdict through the
real dropdowns rather than by poking at internals, visits all three grading sub-tabs, and
reads `document.body.innerText` at each state. The required and forbidden phrase lists are
asserted against that union. Exit code 0 or 1, so it can gate a deploy. 25 checks at the time
of writing.

**Why it was rewritten.** The 13 November check searched the served HTML and reported two
retired phrases as still present. They were in source comments that record what the wording
used to be and why it changed. The app was fine; the check was wrong. A text search over a
self-documenting single-file app cannot tell a comment from a template literal, and a safety
check that cries wolf stops being read.

**What it cannot do, and this is in the script's own header.** A DOM check only proves absence
in the states it visits. A phrase reachable through a combination the script never produces
would pass unnoticed. That limit cannot be engineered away, only narrowed by visiting more
states — so if you add a UI state, add it to the script. A weaker source scan with comments
stripped runs alongside to cover the unvisited states; it is regex-based and therefore
advisory, prints WARN, and never fails the run on its own. Where the two disagree, believe
the DOM.

**It was verified by being made to fail.** A forbidden phrase was injected into rendered
output in a throwaway copy: the check failed, named all twelve states that rendered it, and
exited 1, and the source scan raised WARN on the same phrase. A green check that cannot go
red is not evidence of anything.

### Validating the app against your own data — the tooling now exists
Three gaps were in the way, and the first was serious enough to have wasted the grading
session. **The case log never recorded the app's own prediction.** Rows written from step B
carried `p_infection: null` and `model: 'not run'`, so 200 labelled images would have produced
grader labels with nothing to compare them against.

The fix is *not* to run the model while a grader labels — a grader who can see the app's answer
is being measured for anchoring, not agreement. Step B stays blind. The predictions are
produced afterwards and joined on.

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| `docs/batch-predict.mjs` | drives the **real app** over a folder of images, one CSV row per image |
| `docs/join-labels.mjs` | joins those predictions to an exported case log, one row per label row |

```
python3 -m http.server 8777 --directory dfu
node docs/batch-predict.mjs /path/to/images --scales scales.csv --out predictions.csv
node docs/join-labels.mjs caselog.csv predictions.csv --out merged.csv
```

**Why it drives a browser rather than loading the weights.** What a clinician sees is not the
classifier output: it is the output after centre-crop geometry, the ROI box with its 60 %
margin, four-view TTA, the 0.70 abstention threshold, the tissue model with the colour rule
filling in classes it never saw, and the CIELAB erythema measurement with its distance
transform and contiguity search. Almost none of that is in the weights. A second
implementation would drift, and the comparison would then be against a system nobody uses.

**Scale.** Erythema extent is a distance, so it needs px-per-cm, which normally comes from a
clinician tapping a ruler. Without `--scales` every row reports `needs-scale` and `ery_cm` is
blank — an honest blank, but it means the app's only continuous measurement is missing from
the comparison. Supply `image,px_per_cm` per image, or one `*,38.2` row for a fixed protocol.

**The join is one-to-many and the rows are not independent.** Two graders on one photograph get
the same prediction. `n_graders_for_image` is written on every row and the summary prints how
many images are double-graded. Analyse per image after adjudication, split by `case_id`, never
per label row — 2n label rows treated as 2n observations give intervals about √2 too narrow.

Also added, because they are unrecoverable afterwards: **`app_build`** and **`model_sha`** on
every row (a sha256 over each model's weights and topology, now in all four model cards), and
**`ery_cm` / `ery_censored` / `ery_state`**. `ery_censored` marks a reading that hit the edge of
the 2.5 cm search band — a lower bound, and it must not be averaged as a point estimate. The
build string is now a single `BUILD` constant rendered into the footer, so the footer and the
log cannot disagree about which version produced a row.

### The one field that cannot be recovered later
`pre_app_call` is now the **first** question in step B's examination: *"Your own call on this
wound, before any app output?"* — Infected / Not infected / Not sure.

Everything else in the case log can be filled in on a later pass. A culture comes back in three
days, an outcome in six weeks, and the app's own prediction is joined on by
`docs/batch-predict.mjs` whenever you like. An unaided call cannot: **once a grader has seen
the app's answer for an image, their unaided judgement of that image is gone for good.** If it
is not captured during the blind pass it does not exist.

It is asked first, deliberately, before the other questions walk the grader through the
criteria — what it captures is the clinical gestalt, not the result of the checklist. It
carries `req: []`, which keeps it off the Grading tab (where a clinician is being helped, not
studied) while `renderLabExam()` still shows it in step B (where the grading session happens
and the app deliberately shows no output of its own). It drives no rule and changes no grade.

`post_app_call` is a column with no question behind it. Filling it needs a second reading
session with the predictions joined and visible, and that session can happen at any time. Only
the first half is urgent.

**What this buys you.** With both halves you can report agreement with the reference standard
before and after, and — the figure a reviewer and an ethics committee actually want — **how
often the app changed the call, and in which direction**. That is the number that answers
whether the tool helps or harms, and it cannot be obtained from accuracy figures alone.

## 3. Findings that should not be re-litigated

**The "first in the world" claim will not survive review.** DFUC2021 released 15,683 ulcer
patches labelled for infection and ischaemia five years ago. The defensible claim is narrower
and holds: sign-level infection labels rather than one binary; tissue culture as a **second
independent axis**, not a better truth; Fitzpatrick type per image; Southeast Asian population;
grading derived from published rules rather than predicted. The full argument is in the Google
Doc above.

**The shipped infection score is not a measure of infection.** Its training label is
`slough > 0.10 or necrosis > 0.15`, a colour proxy over the wound bed, with peri-wound erythema
deliberately excluded. AUC 0.834 is against that proxy, never against culture or a clinician's
diagnosis. It is therefore largely collinear with the tissue percentages the same panel
displays, and must never be reported as a second, independent signal. This is the strongest
reason the model does not set the headline, and it is the first thing a reviewer will find.

**Prevalence binds the grading session, not sample size.** At 5 % prevalence kappa sits near
0.41 however many images are labelled, and falls below 0.40 in about 47 % of runs with good
graders. Enlarging the sample does not fix it; enriching it does. 150–200 images at 25–30 %
prevalence per sign, ≥40 positives each, PABAK and the 2×2 table alongside kappa, and **split by
patient, never by image** — keep the `P014_v1.jpg` convention.

**Architecture is not the lever on the tissue model.** U-Net macro 0.603, Attention U-Net 0.598,
DeepLabV3+ 0.572 for 26 % more parameters, all measured the same way. The dataset authors' larger
gap comes from a ~45M-parameter ViT backbone that will not fit a browser. Semi-supervised
learning over the 600 unlabelled images gained 0.01–0.03, inside the fold spread. The one change
that moved slough, 0.438 to 0.495, was fixing a monitored metric that averaged over absent
classes and so selected epochs on whether an untrainable class stayed silent.

## 4. Where the deep-learning work resumes

**The three asked-for signs still have no detector.** Pus, abscess and wet gangrene are supplied
by the examiner. `docs/findings-head-training.md` explains why no colour rule can give them one
and why a transfer-learned head could, on texture, gloss and margin. Abscess is subsurface, so it
must be reported as a likelihood, never a sighting.

**This is the gate.** A findings head needs labelled positives from the graders, negatives
included; an untouched image is excluded from training, so silence is not a negative. That is
what the 150–200 image session is for, and until those labels exist the prototype cannot advance
on the signs that matter clinically.

The session is no longer blocked on the written definitions. All six are in
`docs/case-definitions.md`: the question in one sentence, how to judge it, a table of false
positives with the discriminating feature for each, and what a photograph cannot settle.
Abscess is recorded as a likelihood rather than a sighting, because it is subsurface; wet
gangrene and dry eschar are separate labels, separated on six features, because merging them
merges drainage with revascularisation. **What remains is clinical sign-off**, which has not
been done, and wet gangrene and abscess are the two worth a second opinion before two graders
produce a statistic you intend to publish.

**Slough remains the weak class** at 0.495 cross-validated against 0.711 for granulation. Its
percentages should not be presented to clinicians as reliable. The remaining lever is more
labelled slough, which means your own graders, not another public dataset.

## 5. Design rules embedded in the app

Preserve these through any later change. Each was adopted after a fault found in use.

- **The model may prompt, never conclude — and never de-escalate.** The words "no signs of
  infection" do not exist in the app. States are LIKELY INFECTION, AWAITING YOUR EXAMINATION,
  NOT ENOUGH TO CALL INFECTION, and **only the criteria count or an examiner-confirmed severe
  sign may produce the first of them**. A transfer-learned score at or above 0.5 with the
  criteria unmet produces AWAITING YOUR EXAMINATION and a named prompt, not a diagnosis.
  See v11.2 below for why.
- **The headline may never contradict the rows above it**, and the basis line says why it reads
  as it does — the criteria count first, then the model's number if it is flagging.
- **The headline is the IWGDF/IDSA criteria count**, each criterion named. Slough and necrosis
  are tissue viability and get their own line.
- **The estimate is a percentage below the criteria count**, carrying that it is an estimate from
  training data, not a finding, and not calibrated.
- **Every number names what it actually estimates**, in the words of its own training label,
  and says when it is not independent of something else on screen. A model card holds the label
  rule (`label_rule`) and the UI reads it at render time; no figure or threshold is written
  into the HTML.
- **Every row names its provenance**, and a class the training set never contained stays with the
  colour rule rather than being reported absent.
- **Antibiotic regimens are entered by the study team** from the Malaysian National Antimicrobial
  Guideline, with source, edition and who entered them, gated behind allergy and renal function.
- **Wagner and Texas are computed from the published definitions**, not predicted.
- **The study-team passcode is not a security control** and the code says so.
