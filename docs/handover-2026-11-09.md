**The ROI result is the prototype's strongest internal finding.** Cropping to
the segmented wound before classification lifted severity AUC 0.927 → 0.963 and
accuracy 0.841 → 0.925. The median ulcer occupies about 2.3% of the frame
(`mean_predicted_area_frac` 0.0226), so the whole-frame model was spending most
of its capacity on floor, bedding and skin. That is the argument for
segmentation-before-classification, and it is measured, not asserted.

**Both classifiers are trained on weak labels** derived from FUSeg masks plus
colour features. The model cards say so. They are demonstrators and must not be
quoted as diagnostic performance.

## 3. Two findings that should not be re-litigated

**The "first in the world" claim will not survive review.** DFUC2021 released
15,683 ulcer patches labelled for infection and ischaemia five years ago
(Goyal et al., 2020, *Computers in Biology and Medicine*, Q1). The defensible
claim is narrower and holds: sign-level infection labels (pus, abscess, wet
gangrene) rather than one binary; tissue culture as a **second independent
reference axis**, not a better truth — IWGDF/IDSA define foot infection
clinically, and deep tissue and bone cultures agree only ~52% of the time;
Fitzpatrick type recorded per image; Southeast Asian population; grading derived
from published rules rather than predicted.

**Prevalence binds the grading session, not sample size.** Simulated κ for one
binary sign:

| Prevalence | n=100 | n=400 |
|---|---|---|
| 30% | κ 0.71, CI width 0.30 | κ 0.72, CI width 0.15 |
| 15% | κ 0.63, CI width 0.41 | κ 0.63, CI width 0.20 |
| 5% | κ 0.42, CI width 0.61 | κ 0.41, CI width 0.31 |

At 5% prevalence κ sits near 0.41 however many images are labelled, and falls
below 0.40 in roughly 47% of runs — with good graders. Adding images does not
fix the kappa paradox; enriching the sample does. Recommendation: 150–200
images, enriched to 25–30% prevalence per sign, ≥40 positives each, report
PABAK and the 2×2 table alongside κ, and **split by patient, never by image**
(keep the `P014_v1.jpg` filename convention — the app derives case identity from
the prefix).

## 4. Where the deep-learning work resumes

**The three asked-for signs still have no detector.** Pus, abscess and wet
gangrene are supplied by the examiner. `docs/findings-head-training.md` sets
out why no colour rule can give them one — pus occupies the same 55–105° hue
window the rule assigns to slough, and wet gangrene the same `L < 30` bucket as
dry eschar — and why a transfer-learned head can, on texture, gloss and margin.
Abscess is different in kind: it is subsurface, so a head predicts it from what
travels with it and must be reported as a likelihood, not a sighting.

**This is the gate.** Training a findings head needs labelled positives from the
graders, negatives included — an untouched image is excluded from training, so
silence is not a negative. That is what the 150–200 image session is for. Until
those labels exist, the deep-learning prototype cannot advance on the signs that
matter clinically.

**Tissue segmentation is capacity-limited by data, not architecture.** Measured
the same way: U-Net macro 0.603 (5.93M parameters), Attention U-Net 0.598
(6.14M), DeepLabV3+ 0.572 (7.45M). Attention ties inside the noise; DeepLabV3+
is worse for 26% more parameters. The dataset authors' larger gap comes from a
~45M-parameter ViT backbone, roughly 90 MB in float16 — not deployable in a
browser. At browser-sized capacity the decoder is not what decides this.
Necrosis, pus and pale channels score 0.0 because DFUTissue contains no pixels
of them; they are untrainable on this data, not failing.

**One housekeeping item.** `ml/results/dfutissue-tissue-metrics.json` still
carries the withdrawn figures (granulation 0.774, slough 0.564) from the split
that pooled DFUTissue's TrainVal and Test directories, so about 13 of the 16
official test images were trained on. The deployed card at `dfu/tissue/` records
the supersession; the file in `ml/results/` does not. Anyone reading it would
quote optimistic numbers. It should be annotated or removed.

## 5. Design rules now embedded in the app

These were fixed in response to faults reported from the ward and should be
preserved by any later change.

- **A model may escalate, never de-escalate.** An uncalibrated probability is
  evidence that something is wrong, never evidence that nothing is. The words
  "no signs of infection" are gone. States are LIKELY INFECTION, AWAITING YOUR
  EXAMINATION, NOT ENOUGH TO CALL INFECTION.
- **The headline is the IWGDF/IDSA criteria count**, two or more of five local
  criteria, each named on screen. Slough and necrosis are tissue viability, not
  infection criteria, and get their own line.
- **The basis line says why the headline reads as it does** — "flagged by the
  model, not by the criteria" when the model raised it.
- **The estimate is a percentage below the criteria count**, not above it, so
  the order on screen is the order of the evidence: checkable at the bedside
  first, uncheckable estimate second, carrying the caveat that it is an estimate
  from training data, not a finding on this patient, and not calibrated.
- **Antibiotic regimens are not written into the app.** The study team enters
  them from the Malaysian National Antimicrobial Guideline and they display with
  source, edition and who entered them, gated behind allergy and renal
  function, because empirical choice follows local MRSA and ESBL prevalence.
- **Wagner and Texas are computed from the published definitions**, not
  predicted. Depth separates Texas 1/2/3 and ischaemia sets stage C or D, and
  no photograph shows either — hence the questions before a grade.
- **The study-team tab's passcode is not a security control** and the code says
  so. It must never be treated as one.
