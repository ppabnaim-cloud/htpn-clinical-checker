Part A · Methods decisions

Decision 1Split by patient, not by image

The proposal specifies “a minimum of 2,000 labelled DFU images” and never mentions how many patients those images come from. That single omission is the most consequential gap in the methods section.

A diabetic foot ulcer is photographed at successive visits, and often from more than one angle at a single visit. If a patient contributes six images and a random split scatters them across training, validation and test sets, the test set contains near-duplicates of training images. The model can score well by recognising the patient rather than the pathology, and the reported accuracy is then partly a measure of memorisation. Reviewers of medical imaging papers look for this first, and it is the flaw most often found.

What to write instead

Randomise at the level of the patient, so that every image from one person falls entirely within one split. State the number of patients alongside the number of images at every point in the thesis, and report the mean and range of images per patient. Where a patient is photographed over several visits, decide in advance whether serial images count as independent observations; for the primary analysis they should not.

Decision 2What 2,000 images actually buys

The 2,000-image target is justified in the proposal as “consistent with the benchmark established by the DFUC2020 dataset”. That is a dataset-size convention, not a sample size calculation, and an examiner is entitled to ask what precision it delivers. The calculation is straightforward for the binary infection task, because sensitivity and specificity are simple proportions.

What to write instead

Set the recruitment target in patients, not images: a minimum of 400, with 500 giving comfortable margin at four images per patient. Justify it on the precision of sensitivity and specificity for the binary infection endpoint, and cite the 2,000-image figure only as a secondary check that the dataset is comparable in scale to DFUC2020.

Decision 3Six Wagner grades are out of reach

Grading on the full Meggitt–Wagner scale requires enough examples of every grade, and the rare grades set the recruitment target. Gangrene of the whole foot is perhaps two per cent of ulcers presenting in primary care, and localised gangrene perhaps seven per cent. The arithmetic is unforgiving.

The risk if left unchanged

A six-class model trained on a few hundred patients will have single-figure counts in grades 4 and 5. Per-class recall for those grades will be unreportable, macro-averaged F1 will be dominated by noise, and the examiners will read it as a model that cannot recognise the grades that matter most clinically.

What to write instead

Make the primary endpoint the referral decision, which is binary and is the decision a general practitioner actually faces: manage in primary care, or refer. Operationally that is Wagner 1 against Wagner 2 or above. It needs about 200 patients per arm, which the cohort in Decision 2 already delivers.

Then report a three-band model as the secondary analysis — superficial, deep or infected, and gangrenous — and the full six-class model as exploratory only, with per-class counts shown and no claim made about the rare grades. Pre-specify this hierarchy so it cannot be read as selective reporting after the fact.

Decision 4Wagner 0 is excluded by your own criteria

Wagner grade 0 describes an intact foot: a pre-ulcerative lesion, a healed ulcer, or deformity without breach of the skin. The inclusion criteria require “presence of an active DFU”. The two cannot both hold, so no patient in the planned cohort can carry a grade 0 label.

This matters more than a definitional tidy-up, because the stated purpose is early detection in primary care, and the grade 0 and grade 1 feet are exactly the population early detection is about. As written, the study builds a tool that grades ulcers which have already formed.

Two distinct claims

Detecting and grading an existing ulcer and predicting which foot will ulcerate are different tasks with different reference standards, different designs and different evidence requirements. The proposal currently moves between them. Choose one as the thesis and name the other as future work.

What to write instead

Either drop grade 0 from the label set and state plainly that the model grades established ulceration, which is the cleaner and more defensible thesis; or add a second recruitment arm of at-risk feet without ulceration — previous ulcer, deformity, neuropathy or callus — and accept that this arm has no microbiological reference standard and needs a follow-up outcome instead. The first option is what I would choose for a doctorate with a fixed timetable.

Decision 5Where you recruit is not where you deploy

The methodology recruits “across hospitals”, while the study output describes scale-up “in primary care settings for early detection”. A model trained in hospital wound clinics and deployed in a Klinik Kesihatan meets a different spectrum of disease: milder and earlier ulcers, a different case mix, different cameras in different hands, and staff with less wound-care specialisation. Accuracy measured in the first setting systematically overstates accuracy in the second. This is spectrum bias, and it is the standard reason diagnostic models disappoint on deployment.

What to write instead

Recruit at least one primary-care site into the training cohort, so the training distribution reaches the deployment distribution. Then pre-register the prospective feasibility study as external validation in primary care, state in advance that performance is expected to fall, and report performance broken down by site and by device. A model that holds up across sites is a stronger finding than a higher number from a single centre.

Decision 6Calibration, abstention and reporting standards

The evaluation plan lists AUC-ROC, sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive value, F1 and the Matthews correlation coefficient. Every one of those measures discrimination, which is whether the model ranks infected ulcers above uninfected ones. None measures calibration, which is whether the number it prints is believable.

The distinction is practical. A model can achieve an AUC of 0.85 and still report a 90 per cent probability of infection for ulcers whose true risk is 50 per cent. Since the output is shown to a clinician as a confidence score, and since the proposal links it to antibiotic decisions, the printed number has to mean what it says.

Add three things to the evaluation plan

  • Calibration. Calibration slope and intercept, a calibration plot against observed frequencies, and the Brier score. TRIPOD+AI requires these for any prediction model.
  • Abstention. A confidence threshold below which the tool declines to classify and advises referral. Report the proportion of images declined alongside accuracy on those retained. A tool that admits uncertainty is both safer and more acceptable to clinicians, and it disposes of the out-of-distribution problem, which is what happens when the camera is pointed at something that is not a foot.
  • Inter-rater reliability as a ceiling. The proposal already plans two labellers with consultant adjudication. Report Cohen's or Fleiss' kappa between them, and treat it as the ceiling on achievable model performance. If the two clinicians agree on infection only 80 per cent of the time, a model scoring 85 per cent against their consensus deserves scrutiny, not celebration.

Commit to the reporting guidelines by name

Naming them in the protocol costs a sentence and signals methodological seriousness: TRIPOD+AI for the prediction model, STARD for the diagnostic accuracy component, CLAIM for the imaging specifics, and DECIDE-AI for the prospective feasibility and early implementation stage. Add a completed checklist for each as a thesis appendix.

A worked example from the prototype

The demonstrator built for this project illustrates why the explainability claim needs the same scrutiny. On 190 held-out images from the public FUSeg dataset, the ulcer occupied a median 1.3 % of the photograph, while Grad-CAM resolves nothing finer than about 2 % per cell. The hottest point of the heatmap fell inside the true ulcer in 13 % of images. Adding a segmentation model and classifying the cropped wound raised severity accuracy from 84 % to 92.5 %. Grad-CAM alone is not an explainability strategy; segmentation plus a measured wound area is.

Form 1Image labelling case record form

One form per photographed ulcer episode, completed independently by two trained wound-care clinicians, with a third at consultant level adjudicating disagreement. Fields follow the University of Texas and Meggitt–Wagner systems and the 2023 IWGDF/IDSA infection criteria. Print this section for site use.
